# Research a decision

Research is optional in the delivery workflow. Use it when an unresolved factual question could materially change the idea, product behavior, feasibility, or technical plan. Honor an explicit research request without turning it into an open-ended survey.

## Bound the question

Read the current brief, relevant decisions, and existing findings. State what needs to be learned, what decision it affects, and what evidence would be sufficient. Keep a short question list and respect any supplied time or usage limit. Reuse answered questions rather than researching settled facts again.

Use the main agent by default. Delegate only a bounded question whose concrete benefit justifies the handoff and when delegation is permitted; define its scope and evidence output. A background specialist is not required for routine reading.

## Gather and assess evidence

Prefer the source that owns a claim: official documentation, specifications, source code, first-party APIs, or original research. Inspect the actual source rather than relying on search snippets or a secondary summary. Browse to verify changing or uncertain external facts, following available tools and applicable instructions.

Separate documented facts, measured observations, and your inferences. Note relevant version, date, environment, conflicting evidence, and limits. If a question cannot be answered, describe the remaining uncertainty and its practical effect rather than guessing. A simulated provider response is not evidence of a live integration.

Stop when the important questions have enough evidence for the next decision, or when the agreed limit is reached. Broaden only when a finding exposes a material new uncertainty; explain the change in scope. Do not investigate adjacent topics merely because they are interesting.

## Retain useful findings

Return concise answers, supporting source links, significant limits, and what the findings imply for the decision. Identify which uncertainty remains and whether an experiment or owner choice would help. Research recommendations are inputs to later approval, not new product requirements or permission to implement.

Use the current project's authorized destination. If it keeps research or acceptance evidence in a tracker, use the relevant issue; if it permits research notes, follow that convention. Ordinary lookups can remain in the conversation. With no configured destination or write authority, present findings in the conversation rather than creating a repository notes directory or external issue automatically. Never recreate a parallel backlog or copy another project's IDs, costs, or policies.
